00Stance

Search is a tool held over the room, not a mode that replaces it.

The room's own record says it: you do not browse your history, you go and get something out of it, and search is the front door. A front door is a thing you pass through and forget. The search that shipped was a corridor that rearranged the house: type a word and every column changed what it held; the transcript being read vanished under a list; press a hit and the query was gone, so the second hit cost a retype. The editors got this right two decades ago and never looked back. In WebStorm, VS Code, Spotlight and Raycast, search is summoned (one chord, from anywhere), it floats (the work stays visible and untouched beneath it), it takes one pick and leaves, and it remembers what you asked. That is the whole design, and everything below is those four verbs drawn in this room's material.

Three consequences are worth stating up front. H-2 gets stronger. The record forbade the columns changing geometry under search; the sheet means they change nothing at all, content included. Q5 is answered — both, each with one job. The strip keeps a launcher that states the scope and never takes text; the sheet is the surface. The protocol grows by two fields and the canonical model by none. A hit now carries the row's kind and time, both already in the detached index's own table.

The rules that generalise

  1. Summon from anywhere with one chord; the launcher is the pointer's door to the same place.
  2. Float over the work. The work changes nothing — not content, not geometry, not scroll.
  3. The first row is the cursor the moment rows exist, so Enter lands at once.
  4. Restore the last query only if it is instantly replaceable: preselect its text so typing replaces it and the arrows edit it. (VS Code leaves restore off by default; JetBrains keeps history on a key. This room restores, because the second landing is the commonest act.)
  5. An empty field shows what was searched before, most recent first, forgettable one by one.
  6. Every row is self-labelling: who said it, what matched, when — and a head that never scrolls away places it.
  7. Group by the unit the reader navigates to (a conversation), never by the unit the index ranks (a message) — and give the keyboard a way past a whole group.
  8. The match is drawn as hierarchy, not as a highlighter.
  9. The count replaces the word "results", and a full page says it is the first page.
  10. Filters are chips that say what they narrow, in the reader's words, mapped one-to-one onto the API.
  11. A new keystroke dims the old list; it never blanks it.
  12. The pointer moves the cursor without scrolling the list; the keyboard moves it and scrolls.
  13. Landing is a continuation: the sheet leaves, the work opens, the target is ringed once, and a stepper offers the rest.
  14. Degraded is a sentence with a number, in the foot, never a spinner.

03The sheet, working

Press the launcher or ⌘K inside the frame. Type tokens, row, ramp or debounce — the demo corpus is real-shaped and small. Walk with ↑↓, open with ↵, then ⌘G to the next match. Esc closes and the room is exactly as you left it.

07Landing, and the stepper

A hit is a continuation, not a navigation — unchanged from the record. Pressing a row closes the sheet, opens the project and the thread, and lands the transcript on that message, ringed once in --vf-select. Two things are new.

The landing asks for its row exactly. The work index already resolves an anchor with one session-scoped read (GET …/timeline/{id}); search now takes the same read before any blind page-walk, so a hit forty pages up lands in one request instead of eight. The scroll is the transcript scroller's own, rect-relative, never scrollIntoView.

The stepper. What the sheet leaves behind is a pill in the transcript head where the range caption stood: debounce · 3 of 18 ‹ › ×. Its list is that session's hits in reading order (by time, then id); ⌘G and the arrows walk it with the same landing; × dismisses; changing session dismisses. The launcher keeps the query in primary ink meanwhile, and ⌘K reopens the same rows for the other sessions.

09Deltas

DESIGN.md — none. The sheet is §8's top-attached sheet with a stated width exception; the material, shadow, easing, ring and backdrop are all §5–§7 as written. The width exception is recorded in the room's own record, where the room's geometry lives.

design-agent-history.md — applied 2026-09-09. §6 rewritten from "designed, not promised" to "a tool held over the room" (the transformation retired, the sheet, the head, the row, the stepper, memory); "Search, and where it lives" gains the launcher; §9's scope row now names the launcher and the foot; Q5 answered — both, each with one job.

Coverage — prompts and replies only (decided 2026-09-09, after the first build). Only user and agent rows become search documents; the policy is one predicate in the store, shared by the document visitor, the build denominator and the legacy path, and the sidecar schema bumps to 4 so an existing index rebuilds. The API refuses any other kind and a set is_error rather than answering with nothing. The extractor keeps its lanes for the other kinds, so widening later is one edit.

Protocol v17 — one addition. SearchHit carries kind (the canonical wire vocabulary) and at (milliseconds, or null when the source never said). Both were already columns of the detached index's own document table; the query selects them and the canonical projection gains no column, no table, no journal — iron rule 5 untouched. The wire golden and the TypeScript bindings were regenerated; the app checks the version and asks for a restart.

The app. components/search/ (the sheet, the launcher, the stepper, the shortcut helper), lib/search-sheet.ts (the pure model: lines, cursor, snippet, sentences, memory — 16 tests), styles/search.css, useSearch with facets and a kept previous response, the transcript's exact-anchor landing and rect-relative scroll, and the removal of every column's search mode (projects-with-hits, sessions-with-hits, the hit list, their props and CSS).

10What the running daemon showed, and what is open

  • Verified against a cloned projection on protocol 17. Launcher → sheet → 200 rows under 43 heads → cursor → prompts-only → Enter landed on the exact reply with the ring and 1 of 18 in the head → ⌘G walked to the next → ⌘K reopened with the query preselected and the project chip present → dark → 980px. The frame's scroll stays at 0 after a landing.
  • Coverage, then and now. Before the decision, one ordinary query returned 111 activity rows, 62 replies, 15 thinking rows, 11 team rows and one prompt — four documents in five were machinery, and the index build paid for all of them. Search now indexes prompts and replies only: on a copy of the same corpus, 584,002 rows became 151,705 documents, the index shrank from 1.04 GB to 340 MB, and the forced rebuild reported live progress and was ready 35 seconds after the daemon started.
  • Open — the match markers. FTS5 wraps a match in [ ], and code has brackets of its own; a literal arr[0] reads as a match. The fix is private-use markers on the wire, which changes every consumer's snippet parse and the frozen search campaigns' digests — a deliberate, separate change.
  • Open — literal \n. Some Codex prompts arrive with escaped newlines as text; the sheet shows them as written. Rewriting content is not the sheet's job; if it is anyone's, it is the adapter's.
  • Open — recency. Sessions rank by hit count then last activity. A recent sort is one chip away and was left out to keep the row of chips to three.
  • Open — modifier-Enter. Obsidian's ⌘↵ (new tab), JetBrains' Ctrl+↵ (full window) and VS Code's → (open in the background) all give a second way to open. The natural one here is ⌘↵ to open the session without landing on a message; not built.
  • Open — the unassigned pseudo-project. It names no project the index can select by, so the this project chip is withheld there rather than offered and silently ignored.
