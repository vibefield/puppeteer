Self-Healing Mat

A UI system for the cutting-mat canvas. The mat is a desk under a tree: mid-green, faintly gridded, with leaf shadow moving across it.

Everything placed on it is one of three things: printed on the vinyl, laid on it as paper, or held over it as a tool. One hot orange marks the state that matters right now. The rules below come from the shader itself, not from a template.

What the shader already decided

Six constants in the hero-desk pass fix most of the design before any UI exists. Each one below is quoted, then turned into a rule.

lightDir = normalize(vec3f(1.2, 0.8, -1.2))

Light sits upper-right

Screen-up is world −z, so every cast shadow falls lower-left. The system's elevation shadows all offset (−x, +y).

hsb.g = min(hsb.g + (1.0 − shade) × 0.1, 1.0)

Shadows get richer, not greyer

Darkening adds saturation. Hover and press deepen a colour; nothing in the system goes grey, and there is no neutral grey to reach for.

cover = max(cover, coarse × 0.28)

The mat is printed at 28%

The heaviest grid line is cream at 28% over sage. Anything meant to look printed on the vinyl uses cream at 28% or less, 55% for ruler digits.

pow(color, 2.2) … pow(color, 1/2.2) → LinearSRGB output

The mat renders darker than its swatch

Sage #86a078 in the source lands on screen as #3d5a30, and #2b4220 under leaves. Cream clears 6.8:1 in full light; ink fails at 2.5:1. Text on the mat is always cream.

cell = matSize.x / 350 → 8.3 px at default zoom

Eight pixels a cell, eighty a module

On a 900px-tall viewport a cell is 8.3 px and the heavy line repeats every 83 px. Spacing runs on 8, layout on 80, so components sit on the mat's own lines.

SecondOrderDynamics(f = 1, z = 0.3, r = 2)

The world moves; the tools don't

Leaves breathe at three harmonics and the projector follows the mouse on an underdamped spring. That spring is the only easing the UI borrows; nothing else animates on its own.

Verdict: a desk, not a dashboard

No single treatment survives every job. The system is four of them assigned by role. Print for what the mat says on its own. Paper for what the user makes. Ink for the tools that act on it. Floating cream type for the few words that need no container.

Height sorts them: print is flush, paper rests, tools hover. Ink stays at 94% because anything more transparent turns olive over green, and it never scales with zoom because it is not on the desk. One burnt orange is reserved for state: the selection, the focus ring, the live dot, the active tool. It is never used as text on the mat, where it reads at 1.9:1.

Rules

Do

  • Set text on the mat in cream. Brass only for mono labels at 12px and up.
  • Cast every shadow lower-left, and widen the penumbra as things rise.
  • Snap paper to the 80px module and pad on 8. The mat is the layout grid.
  • Use dashed rules on paper and on ink. Solid lines belong to the mat.
  • Keep hot orange for state: selection, focus, live, the active tool.
  • Put anything that must be read on paper or ink. Print is passive by definition.
  • Let ink chrome stay fixed while the desk zooms underneath it.

Don't

  • No pure white, pure black, or neutral grey anywhere. Whites are cream, blacks are ink, greys are grey-brown.
  • No ink, orange, or brass-mute text on the mat.
  • No ink panel below 90% opacity over the mat. Glass goes olive.
  • No corners above 3px on paper, no square tools. Actions are pills.
  • No ambient UI animation. The leaves are the motion; a live dot may pulse, nothing else.
  • No grid lines inside UI. Tables use dashed leaders so they never compete with the mat.
  • No red. A destructive item warms on hover and the dialog says what it will do.
