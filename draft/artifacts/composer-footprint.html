draft/composer · compact — three shapes for the one fixed object

The capsule spends a quarter of the window on chrome. Three ways to give it back.

At rest the Composer reserves 232px of every room and 338px at its ceiling; in Godview and History that is the bottom third of the panes and the transcript. This page keeps every feature the record lists — the island and its four rooms, the draft and its mention, the five foot controls, the hand and its five faces, dictation, the states, the laws — and lays the same elements out three different ways. Each shape pulls one lever; the numbers below are measured from the rendered object, never typed.

01

The problem

Where the 196 pixels go, and what the rooms lose

The capsule's thesis was a container, not a bar: give the prompt the room to be a paragraph and move everything that is not composition into an island. The cost of that sentence is that the paragraph's room is reserved whether or not there is a paragraph. Of the 196px at rest, the draft's one line is 23px. Everything else is navigation, foot and air.

What the rooms keepin this 740px frame

History's transcript column: 456px of 740 at rest, 350px at the ceiling. Godview's stage, after its own bar and deck: 310px at rest. On a 14-inch window (≈900px of content) the reserve is 26% at rest and 38% while a long prompt is being written — and the reserve is there when nothing is being written at all.

Lever 1 — move the island

The island is 61px of navigation sitting on the composer's head. Every room already has a head line — History's search strip, Godview's instrument bar, the canvas's top HUD cluster. Put the island there and it costs the foot nothing.

Lever 2 — fold the paragraph

Reading and writing are different postures. A capsule that is open only while it is being written in gives every reading room the paragraph's height back, and keeps the writing posture exactly as designed.

Lever 3 — restack the foot

The island, the three ghost controls, the mic and send are all 36–52px tall. One control row can hold them all with air to spare; the draft sits above it, and the capsule is two rows, not three bands.

A fourth lever runs through all three: the hand. Tucked into the foot it costs the foot its whole 54px band; tucked behind the head, or laid on a shelf, it costs nothing until it holds something. And the foot line stops being a band: the mention carries the transport, and a status line unfolds only when it has a sentence (S1).

02

Three shapes

The same elements, laid out three ways

Nothing is removed. The island keeps its 486px and its four rooms, keys, accessory and create; the draft keeps its ceiling and its mention; the foot keeps attach, aim, @, mic and send with their three weights; the hand keeps its five faces and Quick Look. What changes is where each lives, and what the room gets back.

D1 · Head and footisland on the room's head line · a 52px line at the foot · grows to ≈196

Navigation up, composition down. The island leaves the capsule and takes the room's head line, where every room already keeps a strip — History's search, Godview's instrument bar, the canvas's top HUD. The composer becomes one 52px line: the three ghost controls, the draft, the mic and send, with the controls pinned to the bottom as the draft grows. Its ceiling is where today's capsule starts.

The hand tucks behind the head. Cards stand behind the line's top edge and show a 42px tip — the mark row and one whole line, so no label is sliced mid-letter; the magnet lifts them up into the room instead of down into the foot. The reserve grows by the tip only while the hand holds something.

Two consequences fall out. The tray is now the island opening into a sheet from the head (M1's letter — Q3 dissolves, because the composer no longer morphs). And Godview's bar shares its line with the island, which is the answer Q8 was waiting for.

D2 · The foldone object, two postures · closed 52 · open = today's capsule

Open while you write, closed while you read. Nothing about the writing posture changes: ⇧⇧ or a click opens today's capsule exactly as recorded. What is new is a reading posture — the island with a tail: the target, the draft's first line, and how many cards are in hand, on one 52px row. Escape folds it, send folds it; nothing folds it by surprise, because a room that reflows under your pointer is worse than a capsule that stays.

The fold is the morphing-island signature, literally. One element grows from the pill into the paragraph on the island curve and settles back. The closed row is, to the pixel, the shipped HUD island plus a preview — so the product's current object is the reading posture of this one.

It gives back the most while reading and nothing while writing. In History that is most of the time. In Godview a long prompt still reserves 338px, as it does today.

D3 · The stackone object, one posture · a draft row over one control row · ≈104

The learned shape, with the island in the control row. Text above, controls below — the composer Claude, ChatGPT and Slack have all converged on. The island keeps its pill and sits between the two foot clusters, so the row reads as three groups: put something in · where am I and what does this room do · speak and send. The hand tucks behind the head as in D1.

No modes, nothing relocated, everything visible. It is the conservative shape: the thesis, the signature and the laws all hold as written, the island still rides the capsule, and the capsule still grows with the draft.

It gives back the least. The island's row is still inside the capsule, so the floor is the island plus one line. That is the honest price of keeping the object whole.

Considered and not taken. A side rail in the column rooms (the composer as a 320px right-hand column) — Godview and History are wide, but the object would change shape per room and CB-3 dies. A follower (the line docks to the aimed pane in Godview) — a good Godview and a bad travel story; it can be added to D1 later as a behaviour, not a shape. An island that collapses to the current room's mark and unfolds on hover — hidden navigation is not intuitive; keep for narrow windows only. And simply shrinking today's numbers — 196 → 150 buys 46px and keeps every band.

A redesign that only saves pixels is a diet. Two of the three also close open questions the record left for James; one closes none and bends nothing.

08

Verdict

Head and foot, with the hand behind the head

D1 gives the rooms back the most, adds no mode, and matches the mental model every other tool has taught — navigate at the top, write at the bottom. Its cost is a sentence in the thesis, and what it buys with that sentence is three closed questions. D2 is the right answer if the island must stay in the capsule; D3 is the right answer if nothing may move at all.

Recommend — D1, tucked hand, S1

The island on the head line in all four rooms; a 52px line at the foot that grows to today's floor; the hand behind the head; the status line that unfolds only with a sentence. Reserve at rest 88px against 232. If the shelf reads calmer in the hardware eyeball, take it — it is a switch, not a rebuild.

If the island must stay — D2 over D3

The fold keeps the recorded capsule intact and gives History its reading room. Build the fold rule strictly (Esc · send · ⇧⇧) and put the hand's count in the closed row. D3 is the fallback if a posture is unacceptable: no modes, 40% back, nothing bent.
