Inside the rim: four bodies, one line, a gauge, and the brood.

The field. The swarm is a physics field. Every body is a circle with a four-pixel gap, a force pulls all of them to the centre, and left alone they settle into one touching cluster. They can be dragged, and they collide. The first pass hung marks outside the circle, captions beneath, orbits around, halos and pins and tails; in the cluster that space is a neighbour's body, so those marks would sit on another agent or vanish under it. The rule. Everything an agent says, it says inside its own rim. Where a mark needs room, the body itself grows, so the collision circle and the picture stay the same thing. Every design below obeys it; the field at the end is packed the way gravity packs it. Fifth pass. The burn becomes a line graph across the whole body: the last ten minutes of the rate, the present at the right edge, so the body says not only how hard it burns but how that has been going. The speed meter stays as the other reading. The brood becomes a stack of small circles at the bottom of the body, overlapped like a hand of cards, masked by the rim, compact and still countable, each raised by its effort and lettered with its model.

Part I

The bodies

Three sizes become two. The terminal keeps the smallest body; every agent, whatever its state, takes the working one. State is carried by the body's fill, its rim, its ignition, and what sits inside it.

01

The empty terminal

No agent yet. The body says two things at rest, that it is empty and which folder it stands in, and a third when a command runs: what is running, and for how long. The folder is the session's working directory; the command is the foreground process the daemon reports, never the screen. All three designs carried forward from the first pass unchanged, because all three already lived inside the rim.

A

The hollow

recommended

No fill: a dashed ring, the folder mark, the name, and empty beneath. A running command makes the ring solid and sends one arc round it, and takes the bottom lines with its elapsed time. An agent arriving fills the hollow. In the cluster a hollow reads as a gap in the mass.

B

The tile

The shape says it: a rounded square is a terminal, a circle is an agent. Physics can carry it as a chamfered rectangle, and it packs differently against circles, which is either the point or the problem. Prompt glyph as watermark, path under the name, a travelling bar when a command runs.

C

The prompt

The body's copy is a prompt: the folder, a chevron, a blinking cursor. A command completes the line and the cursor becomes three dots with the elapsed time under it. The circle and the grey stay exactly as shipped.

02

Idle, a reply waiting

The idle agent keeps the shipped body and needs a sign that it finished a turn since the pane was last looked at. The tail from the first pass is withdrawn: it stood outside the rim. Three marks inside the body, shown at one reply and at three.

A

The badge

recommended

A badge at one o'clock inside the fill, in the body's text colour. One reply is a dot; more carry the count. Familiar, quiet, and in the cluster it sits in the body's own upper right where no neighbour can cover it.

B

The ring

A 2px ring just inside the rim in the text colour, and the count as a line in the copy. A body holding something for you wears a ring; the terminal's dashed ring and the blocked body's dashed rim are its cousins.

C

The beat

The rim swells inward and back every two seconds, an inset ring breathing from nothing to 3px, with the count in the copy. Motion carries it; among still idle bodies the one that beats is the one that spoke.

03

Working, at its burn

Three readings of the runtime's tokens per minute on the same log scale, 500 to 50,000, each shown at an ember, a steady burn, and a blaze. The first is a history; the other two are instruments. All live inside the rim, and the ignition retires in all three.

A

The graph

recommended

A line across the whole body, behind the copy: the last ten minutes of the burn, one sample every fifteen seconds from the runtime's usage samples, the present at the right edge with a breathing point, the area beneath it washed in the project colour. Height is the rate on the log scale, so an ember lies low and a blaze climbs to the top; the dotted line is the redline, and the point turns orange above it. The body says how hard it burns and how that has been going, in one glance, with nothing that moves but the point.

B

The dial

A speed meter on the inside of the rim: a 240° scale from eight o'clock over the top to four, ticked every ten degrees, a short needle in the text colour with the project colour at its tip, and a redline in orange over the last fifth. The scale opens at the bottom where the context figure lives. The reading is a position, the way a tachometer is read.

C

The tach

The same scale as a bar of twenty segments that light from eight o'clock as the rate climbs, the last four in orange. No needle to read against a tick, just how much of the arc is lit. The digital face of the dial.

04

Blocked, held at size

The blocked body comes down from radius 70 to the working 50 and keeps its inversion. Attention has to come from a mark, and the mark has to be inside the rim: the halo moves in, the pin is withdrawn. The runtime knows why it is blocked, permission, a question, or a failure, so the mark says so. Each design shown for the three kinds.

A

The inner halo

recommended

The rim in orange, and a 2px orange ring 4px inside it, pulsing at the blocked shadow's tempo. The kind is an orange line in the copy. Orange is already the system's one state colour, for a hot context and a retry; blocked is the third thing that needs the human. Nothing leaves the body.

B

The cut

No colour. A dashed ring in the text colour turns slowly just inside the rim, and the watermark changes from the provider mark to the question or the exclamation. The kind is the copy line. A held body, read in monochrome.

C

The flare

breaks rule 1

The whole body goes orange, white copy on it, the kind in white. The loudest possible mark and the easiest to find in a cluster of greys. It spends colour as a ground, which the system reserves for identity, so it is here as the honest extreme of the scale, not as a recommendation.

Part II

The line

One line of text per agent, from the runtime's activity: its kind, its line, and when it began. The caption and the callout from the first pass are withdrawn; both stood outside the rim. Three ways to say it inside a 100px circle.

05one line of activity
05

One line of activity

The kind is a glyph, the line is the text. Shown for a tool call, reasoning, a reply being written, and a blocker's own line. The line brightens when it changes and settles to 45% after six seconds; the full text is the body's title on hover and the inspector's first line.

A

The inner line

recommended

In the model line's place while the agent works; the model returns when it rests. Fourteen characters before the clip, so it is a hint, not a sentence, but it costs nothing and reads in the same glance as the name.

B

The arc

Set along the inner rim's lower half, like the legend on a coin: 160° of arc gives about twenty-six characters at 7px. The context figure moves up above the name. The most text the circle can hold without touching the copy, and the least conventional to read.

C

The two lines

The name drops to 10.5px and the line wraps to two at 7.5px beneath it, about thirty characters. The model line hides while the agent works. A fuller sentence, at the price of a busier body.

Part III

The gauge

Context used is the one number the user watches climb. Today it is a 10% wash from the floor and a six-pixel figure. Three ways to make it a mark inside the body, each turning orange at 80% as the deck's gauge edge does.

06context, legible
06

Context, legible

Shown at 12%, 62% and 91%, on a working body. Where the provider reports no percentage the mark is absent and the figure reads two dashes; a derived percentage is drawn the same as a reported one, and the inspector says which it was. The ring from the first pass moves from outside the rim to on it.

A

The ring

recommended

The rim is the gauge: a 3px arc from twelve o'clock in the project colour along the inside of the rim, over a hairline track, orange from 80%, the figure at 8.5px on the bottom line. The deck's edge, bent round the body, on the body.

B

The tide

The shipped fill, doubled to 20% and given a waterline: a one-pixel line across the body at the level. The body fills like a vessel, orange when it nears the brim. Keeps the current idea and makes it visible.

C

The dial

A 220° arc inside the body, filling from the left like a speedometer, the figure inside it. Stays clear of the rim, so it can share the body with a ring that means something else; it competes a little with the copy.

Part IV

The brood

An agent that delegates has children. Some are subagents that live and die inside its turn; some are teammates, agents in their own right; some belong to a workflow with a name. The user needs the count at a glance and, one step closer, each child's model and effort. The picture is a stack of small circles at the bottom of the body, overlapped like a hand of cards and masked by the rim, so the brood takes one compact strip and is still counted by its edges. Each card is lettered with its model and raised by its effort, a low card tucked at the rim and a max card lifted into the body, the way a chosen card is lifted from a hand. The context figure moves to the top line to make room. What is left to decide is how the hand is held.

07model and effort08subagents
07

The card, at size

One small circle per child, radius nine. Live children are filled in the body's text colour with a rim in the project colour; finished ones are hollow; a teammate's rim is dashed; the last card counts what did not fit. The model is a letter, F, O, S, H, or the provider's mark. The effort is the raise: how far the card lifts above the rim, two pixels for low and eleven for max.

The raise, shown against the rim line: low, medium, high, max. A card that is done sinks back to low whatever its effort was, so the raised cards are the ones at work.

08

Subagents

The stack on the same three parents: a working agent with four subagents, two of them live; a workflow with nine children, more than fit; and a team lead with two teammates. Six cards show and the seventh counts the rest; the deck shows them all. The parents burn at the graph; their activity line says they are delegating.

A

The hand

recommended

Cards in a row at the bottom, each overlapping the last by half, later cards on top, arrival order left to right. The letter sits in the crescent each card keeps; a live card is raised by its effort and finished cards sit low, so the working children stand up out of the hand. Counted by the edges; forty-four pixels wide at most.

B

The fan

The same cards fanned round a pivot below the body, so they follow the curve of the rim and the letters tilt with them. Raised cards push out along their own radius. The most like a hand of cards, and it takes a little more of the bottom arc.

C

The deck

One pile, each card two pixels behind the last, the newest live child on top with its letter and its raise, the rest showing only as edges, and the count beside the pile. The most compact by far, thirty-six pixels for nine children; the model of everything but the top card waits for the deck legend.

Part V

Together

The recommended set in one field, the hollow, the badge, the graph, the inner halo, the inner line, the ring, the hand, both themes fixed, settled by the same rule the app's physics uses: a pull to the centre and no overlap, with the four-pixel gap. Nothing here is placed by hand.

The cluster

Two terminals, one running a test. An idle agent with a reply waiting. A working agent at a steady burn, 62% through its context, editing a file. A blocked agent asking for permission. A working agent with a hand of four. A second idle agent, resting, and a second working one. Eight bodies, settled.

☼ Light. The hollow terminals are the gaps in the mass; the working bodies carry their graphs; the blocked body is the only one with colour on it, and it is found in a glance.

◐ Dark. The same settlement. Every mark stays with its body when it is dragged, because every mark is part of it.

Decisions

Seven choices, one recommendation each. Picks fold into the record's §10 as the swarm's second pass.

11

What to decide

  1. The empty terminal. The hollow, the tile, or the prompt. Recommended: the hollow, because an agent arriving fills it, and a hollow reads as a gap in the cluster.
  2. A reply waiting. The badge, the ring, or the beat. Recommended: the badge, familiar and quiet, in the body's own upper right.
  3. Burn. The graph, the dial, or the tach. Recommended: the graph, the only reading that carries the history as well as the present.
  4. Blocked. The inner halo, the cut, or the flare. Recommended: the inner halo, orange being the system's one colour for what needs the human; the cut's watermark glyph could ride along.
  5. The line. The inner line, the arc, or the two lines. Recommended: the inner line; the arc if the fuller text is worth the reading.
  6. Context. The ring, the tide, or the dial. Recommended: the ring, on the rim, where the deck's edge already lives.
  7. Subagents. The hand, the fan, or the deck. Recommended: the hand, compact, countable, every card lettered, the working ones raised.
